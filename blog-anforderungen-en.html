The Underrated Success Factor: Clean Requirements

26 June 2026 · RAIT Solution

Most IT projects do not fail because of technology — they fail because of unclear, contradictory or missing requirements. Requirements engineering is the key that decides whether a project succeeds or fails.

Why poor requirements are so expensive

When requirements are unclear, teams build the wrong thing. That sounds obvious — yet it is the most common cause of cost overruns, missed deadlines and dissatisfied clients. A requirement that is misunderstood in the concept phase causes defects that run through the entire development process.

The result: rework, scope creep and a go-live date that keeps slipping further back.

What makes a good requirement

Professional requirements management ensures that everyone involved has the same understanding. Good requirements are:

  • Complete — all relevant aspects are captured
  • Unambiguous — no room for interpretation
  • Testable — it is clear when a requirement has been met
  • Consistent — no contradictions with other requirements
  • Prioritised — what matters most is implemented first

AS-IS, TO-BE and the blueprint

Structured requirements management starts with an analysis of the current state (AS-IS): How do processes work today? Where are the weaknesses? Building on this, the target picture (TO-BE) is developed: How should things work in future?

The blueprint brings both together: it defines the target process, the target architecture and the path to get there — as a binding foundation for the entire implementation.

Functional and technical specifications: the underrated duo

The functional specification sets out what the client wants — from their perspective, in their language. The technical specification translates this into the technical detail the delivery team needs. Together, the two documents create commitment and protect both sides from misunderstandings.
