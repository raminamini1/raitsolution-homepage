AI in Business: Opportunities, Risks and First Steps

Artificial intelligence is transforming industries and business models. Those who fail to act now will fall behind. Those who act unprepared will waste budget. The right path lies in between.

Why AI projects fail so often

Most failed AI initiatives have one thing in common: they start with the technology instead of the problem. Teams experiment with AI tools without first defining clear business goals and measurable success criteria. The result is pilot projects that go nowhere and investments without ROI.

The right starting point: use cases, not technology

Good AI projects start with the question: Which concrete business problem do we want to solve? Only once that question has a clear answer does the technology question arise. Possible AI use cases in organisations include:

  • Automating recurring tasks in case processing
  • Intelligent document processing and classification
  • Anomaly detection in processes and systems
  • Supporting decision-making through data analysis
  • Customer communication and self-service with AI assistants

A data strategy as the foundation

AI is only as good as the data it is trained on. Many organisations underestimate the effort required for data quality, data availability and GDPR-compliant data use. Starting without a solid data strategy means building on sand.

EU AI Act: what organisations need to know now

With the EU AI Act, binding requirements for the use of AI in Europe apply from 2025/2026 — depending on the risk profile of each application. High-risk AI (e.g. in HR, lending or critical infrastructure) is subject to strict requirements for transparency, documentation and human oversight.

Compliance is not an obstacle — it is a competitive advantage for organisations that want to use AI sustainably.
